# Changelog

Entries describe repository changes. Tagged releases are published as GitHub releases and the app's update page shows the top of the Unreleased section of the tagged commit.

## Unreleased

**Screen sharing now works with the official TeamSpeak client, both ways.**

- Watch the official client's share inside Mirage, with its sound, volume, mute and fullscreen.
- Share a whole screen or a single window, in four quality levels up to 1080p60, and optionally send the computer's sound (everything except Mirage).
- Fixed: freezes of about 20 s while watching, a crash when a share was started again, and nicknames must have 3 to 30 characters.
- Known limits: a very slow connection to the server (for example some VPNs) makes the official client ignore your share; the sound goes to everyone through your voice, so a server-muted microphone silences it.

### Details

- **Share the computer's sound.** The share picker has "Enviar o som do computador". Like the official client, the sound travels inside the Opus voice channel (the WebRTC audio section stays inactive; `setupstream` announces `audio=1`). `ts6_audio::loopback` captures WASAPI process loopback excluding Mirage's own process tree (so the call's voices are never sent back), `ts6_audio::system_audio` queues it, and the microphone pipeline mixes it into every frame in a new stream mode: the microphone is heard only while push-to-talk is held (or always without a key), the stream sound passes either way, a voice gate at -55 dBFS replaces the downstream push-to-talk gate, and a computer without a microphone sends the sound over silence. Verified on this Windows 10 (build 19045): sound from an unrelated process is captured while the excluded process is not, and a real end-to-end test turned a tone into 187 Opus voice packets with a key bound and not pressed and no microphone. Not verified: that the official client plays it as the sharer's voice. Limits: with the microphone muted on the server the voice is dropped and so is the sound; the whole computer's sound is sent, not one application; it stops when the share stops.
- **Quality choice for sharing, and a crash fix.** The share picker has four presets: Leve (720p30, 1.5 Mbit/s), Padrão (720p30, 3 Mbit/s, what shares used before), Alta (1080p30, 6 Mbit/s) and Máxima (1080p60, 9 Mbit/s); `share_start` takes `quality`. Every preset was encoded on this machine's hardware encoder (ignored test `quality_presets`); the 1080p levels declare H.264 level 3.1 in the SDP and were not watched from the official client. While measuring, starting a capture for the second to fourth time in one process crashed with an access violation inside `windows-capture` (a cached WinRT factory outliving its COM multithreaded apartment); `ScreenCapture::start` now keeps the apartment alive with `CoIncrementMTAUsage`, and the test starts and stops captures repeatedly (10 in a row passed). This affected stopping and restarting a share in the app.
- **Share one window instead of a whole monitor.** The share picker lists screens and open windows (title, app, size; minimized and shell-hidden windows are left out). `ts6-capture` gained `CaptureSource` (monitor or `HWND`), `list_windows` and capture through Windows Graphics Capture's window path; the bridge command `share_start` takes `window` (a handle) next to `monitor`. Checked on the real desktop with an ignored test that encoded a browser window (first unit a keyframe); not yet watched from the official client. If the window closes the share ends.
- **Stream viewer controls.** While watching a screen share the stage has a volume slider (0-200%), a mute button and a fullscreen button (also double click; Esc leaves). Volume and mute live in `ts6_audio::stream_audio` (atomics, applied in the playback callback) and reset when Mirage restarts. The corner glyphs are painted, so no icon font change. Unit-tested (gain, clamp, mute) and photographed in the real window against a local server (viewer, controls, fullscreen and Esc).
- **Backend: why a connection ended, and channel passwords.** When the session ends because of a connection error the status the frontends receive now says why (`connection closed: <library error>`) instead of a bare "connection closed". `join` takes an optional `password` (sent as `cpw` on `clientmove`, only when non-empty) and snapshot channels carry `has_password`; frontends do not ask for the password yet. Unit-tested only: no live server run, and what the library reports for a kick or a ban was not observed.
- **Server administration.** Right-click a channel to create a subchannel (with an optional password), edit or delete it; right-click a person to poke, kick (from the channel or the server) or ban them; the sidebar's "Administração" opens a window with the server groups, each group's permissions (change a value, remove, set one by name) and the ban list. Channels with a password show a lock and ask for it when you join. Behind it is one typed `admin` bridge command (`ts6_core::admin::AdminOp`: channel create/edit/delete/move, kick, ban, ban list/remove, move, poke, server edit, privilege key, server and channel groups, and permissions of groups, channels and clients); arguments are validated locally and the server decides, so a refusal comes back with the missing permission's number and is shown in Portuguese. The list replies are handed up by a small patch of the vendored `tsclientlib` (`client/UPSTREAM.md`). Checked against a throwaway loopback server with `scripts/check_admin.py` (refusal without rights, privilege key, 8 groups and 281 permissions listed, channel create/edit/permission/delete, move, poke, ban, ban list and removal) and in the real window (menus, dialogs, groups and permissions, a protected channel created and joined with its password, a kick). Not covered: creating or deleting groups, assigning people to groups from the UI, client and channel permission screens (the commands exist), per-channel-group management, and a server that grants less than the first administrator's rights.
- **Private and server-wide messages.** `chat` takes `scope` (`channel`, the default, `server` or `private`) and, for private, `client`; incoming `chat` events carry `scope`, `from_id` and, for private ones, `peer_id` (the sender, or who we wrote to) and an empty `channel`. A recipient who is not on the server (or is you) is refused locally with `stale_client`. In the native client, right-click a person → "Mensagem privada" (or pick "Todo o servidor") in the "Para" row above the composer; private and server-wide messages show in the room's history tagged "privado", "privado para X" or "servidor", are not saved to disk, and attachments still go to the channel only. Checked with `scripts/check_direct_chat.py` against a throwaway server (private both ways, server-wide as an administrator, missing recipient refused, channel message unchanged) and in the real window (menu, target chip, sent and received tags). A default guest may be refused a server-wide message by the server (`not_confirmed`). Not covered: a per-person conversation list or unread badge, notifications, history across restarts, and the browser frontend (it ignores these messages).
- **Away status and a person's server groups.** The `away` bridge command (`away` true/false, optional `message` up to 80 bytes) sends `clientupdate client_away`; snapshots carry `away` for every client. In the native client, clicking your name in the sidebar's user card offers "Ficar ausente" / "Ficar disponível", and people who are away show dimmed with "(ausente)". The person menu has "Grupos do servidor", a dialog of the server's regular groups with a check box each (add or remove that person; it reads `server_groups` and `client_server_groups` and sends `server_group_add_client` / `server_group_remove_client`); it needs the person's database id, which the desktop protocol now reads. Checked live: the away flag round trip between two bridges, the menu and the away tag in the real window, the groups dialog against a throwaway server as an administrator. Not covered: an away message typed in the UI, automatic away after idling, creating or deleting groups, channel groups and per-person permission screens.
- **Private message alert.** A private message from someone you are not already writing to shows a toast, "Mensagem privada de X", for 10 seconds with a "Responder" button that points the composer at that person; a window that is not in front also asks for attention on the taskbar. Checked in the real window against a throwaway server (toast, answer, the reply reached the sender). Not covered: sound, a per-person unread count, and an alert while the window is minimized beyond the taskbar request.
- **Reconnecting by itself.** When the connection to a server is lost (a timeout, a network drop, a server shutdown) the native client tries to come back: up to 8 attempts, waiting 3, 6, 12, 24 and then 30 seconds, shown on the connect card as "Reconectando a <servidor> (tentativa N de 8)" with a "Cancelar" button. A kick or a ban is never retried, leaving on purpose does not retry, and choosing another server cancels it. The rules (`app/reconnect.rs`) are unit-tested; checked in the real window by killing a local server and starting it again (the app showed attempt 5 of 8, then returned to the room on its own). After a reconnection it goes back to the channel you were in when that channel still exists, is not locked and you are not already there. It sends the server password the session was joined with (kept in memory only, never read from the form), stops at once when the server refuses for good (ban, wrong password, identity level) and "Cancelar" also leaves a server an attempt had already reached. Found by an independent review of the branch. Not covered: keeping the draft, the browser frontend, and a long outage (it gives up after about three minutes).
- **Editing a channel sends only what changed.** The edit dialog used to repeat the channel's name even when only the topic changed, which a server may refuse as "name in use"; it now sends the changed fields only and nothing at all when nothing changed (found by the same review; not reproduced on a TS6 server).
- **Why you were removed.** When a kick, a ban, a shutdown or a timeout ends the session the status now says so and the hub shows it in Portuguese ("Você foi expulso do servidor por Ana."). Checked live with a kick.
- README, compatibility matrix and crate notes no longer say screen sharing is unimplemented or untested against the official client.
- **Stream audio is played.** Watching a screen share now decodes the stream's stereo Opus track (with concealment of short gaps), downmixes it to mono and mixes it into the playback next to voice (`ts6_audio::stream_audio`, 60 ms prebuffer, 300 ms cap). The loopback test now sends a 440 Hz tone and asserts it reaches the playback mix. No separate volume or mute for stream audio yet, and it is not verified against the official client with real sound. The loopback viewer test also repeats its clip until the connection is up (it failed about 1 run in 3 when ICE took longer than the 3 s clip).
- Viewer: the decode queue holds 30 access units instead of 4. With the packet window fixed, the log showed no lost packets but short freezes when keyframes (slow to decode) filled the 4-slot queue, which forced a wait for a keyframe the official client rarely sends.
- **Fix freezes of about 20 s while watching a stream.** The viewer reassembled frames with a 64-packet window, smaller than a 1080p keyframe, so every keyframe after the first was dropped and the picture stayed frozen until the sender produced another one by itself. The window is now 4096 packets with a 500 ms bound; a unit test packs a 250 KB keyframe into about 210 packets. Confirmed against the official client: no freezes in a 2-minute run.
- Desktop: saved profiles whose nickname is shorter than 3 characters are tagged "inválido" in the profile list and a red hint says so under the list.
- SDP shaping: the official client accepted only the highest layout (level 5, with `rtcp-xr`/`rrtr`) as an answer, so `next_level` no longer cycles and always writes level 5 (`MIRAGE_SDP_LEVEL` still pins another). Without it the official publisher never applied the Mirage viewer's answer, which is why watching its screen stayed on "Conectando à transmissão…". Measured from the official log (one accepted answer, two refused), not yet confirmed by a fresh run.
- **Screen share against the official client: two causes found in its log.** (1) It only decodes H.264 High (`640c1f`); the publisher offered Baseline (`42e01f`), so it connected but never showed a picture. The offer is now `640c1f` and the encoder requests High (verified: real capture emits `profile_idc=100`). (2) The viewer's NV12 to RGBA conversion took about 70 ms per 1080p frame, which capped playback near 14 fps and froze it through dropped keyframes; it is now fixed point over up to 4 threads (about 1.9 ms). Viewer connection state and decoder throughput are logged at info level; `MIRAGE_LOG_FILE=<path>` writes the log to a file because the desktop exe has no console. Not verified: a full run against the official client after these fixes; whether the stream still drops.
- **Nicknames need 3 to 30 characters.** The server answers `ParameterInvalidSize` outside that range (measured against a loopback server: 1 and 2 characters and 31 refused; 3, 30 and 30 emoji accepted; counted in characters after trimming, not bytes). `ts6_core::check_nickname` is used by the bridge on connect and rename, and the desktop blocks Conectar, the profile dialog and the Ajustes rename with a pt-BR message (a saved profile that is too short also blocks connecting until edited).
- **Experiment, unverified against the official client:** the official TeamSpeak client logs `SetRemoteDescription CALL … OK` for its own SDP but never for ours, in both directions, so screen share did not work with it either way. `ts6-stream` now rewrites our SDP to the Chrome layout (`sdp.rs`, six cumulative levels cycled per negotiation, or pinned with `MIRAGE_SDP_LEVEL`), offers an inactive Opus section and a send-only video section like the official publisher, and uses the m-line index as candidate `mid`. Chromium accepts every level; the official client is untested. Details and how to read the result in `docs/persistent-chat-and-streaming-research.md`.
- Screen share and viewer peers now gather ICE candidates with the three STUN servers the official TeamSpeak client uses (`ts6_stream::configuration`; loopback-bound peers use none), so a connection across two home networks has a chance. Not tested across a NAT yet; no TURN relay.
- **Screen sharing connected to the TeamSpeak session (stage 3).** The desktop call controls now have a working share button: it opens a monitor picker (pt-BR), then the bridge starts the monitor capture and hardware H.264 encoder, announces the stream with `setupstream` (type 2, P2P, no audio) and, for every `notifyjoinstreamrequest`, builds a `ts6-stream` publisher whose offer goes out in `respondjoinstreamrequest`; the viewer's answer and candidates return through `streamsignaling`. The picture is captured and encoded once and the same units go to every connected viewer (at most 4, further requests get `decision=0`); a viewer that joins gets the newest keyframe at once, and no delta is sent before a keyframe. Leaving the channel, a disconnect, a server-side stop or a capture failure ends the share (`stopstream` when the stream id is known). New bridge command/event pair: `share_start { monitor }` / `share_stop` and `share_state { state, reason, viewers }`. `ts6-core` now parses `notifystreamclientjoined`/`notifystreamclientleft` and the `is_remove` flag. **Verified:** unit tests of the signaling state machine, the publisher worker against a loopback `ts6-stream` viewer, and (ignored test, run once on one machine) the real monitor capture and hardware encoder through the worker to a loopback viewer. **Not verified:** any run against a real TeamSpeak server, any official-client viewer, a second machine or NAT, the picker window on screen, audio (none is sent). The native viewer in this repository still does not connect to the official client, so share interoperability with it is an open risk of the same kind. A perfectly still screen produces no new units after the first keyframe.
- **Installer and in-app updates.** Mirage now ships as a per-user Windows installer built with Velopack (`scripts/pack_installer.ps1`, animated splash and icon in `installer/`, assets drawn by `scripts/make_installer_assets.py`). The new `release.yml` workflow packs and publishes a GitHub release when a `v*` tag is pushed (optionally to another repository through the `RELEASES_REPO` variable and `RELEASES_TOKEN` secret); `build.yml` packs the installer on every run. The desktop app calls `VelopackApp::build().run()` first in `main`, checks the public releases page once shortly after start (a toggle in Ajustes turns it off; stored as `skip_update_check`), and updates from Ajustes → Atualizações (download, then "Reiniciar e atualizar"; deltas when available). A portable copy cannot update itself and links to the installer. `MIRAGE_UPDATE_URL` points an installed app at another feed or a local folder; debug builds accept `MIRAGE_DEV_UPDATE=available|uptodate|failed|checking|downloading` to preview the page. Verified locally end to end (install 0.1.0, update to 0.1.1 from a folder feed, restart, uninstall) with a debug build; not verified against GitHub, a signed binary, or a published release. Installers are unsigned (SmartScreen warns). See `docs/RELEASING.md`.
- Native desktop Ajustes redesign: a larger two-pane window (940x660, shrinks to fit small windows) with a page list (Perfil, Áudio, Atualizações, Sobre), a title and one-line description per page, settings as labelled rows instead of a cramped stack, device pickers at a readable width, and an "Atualizações" page with a horizon line and sun that moves while checking, fills while downloading and rests when ready. The gear in the sidebar shows a green dot while an update waits and opens straight to that page. Fixes the old window's cramped height, mixed "Headset" wording (now "Saída de áudio") and misaligned rows.
- New `ts6-capture` crate: captures a monitor with Windows Graphics Capture, scales and converts it to NV12 on the GPU, and encodes it with a hardware Media Foundation H.264 encoder into Annex-B units (low latency, no B-frames, keyframes carry SPS/PPS, a full consumer queue drops a unit and forces the next keyframe). Verified on one machine end to end into the `ts6-stream` publisher and a loopback viewer (`cargo test -p ts6-capture --test screen_to_viewer -- --ignored`). Limits: hardware encoders only, primary or indexed monitor only, a perfectly still screen produces no new units, not yet connected to the TeamSpeak session or tested against the official client.
- New `ts6-stream` crate: native WebRTC publisher and viewer that speak TeamSpeak 6's `streamsignaling` JSON (answer, ICE candidates). Proven on loopback with a synthetic H.264 source (RTP crosses real UDP); not yet connected to the TeamSpeak session, to a screen capture or to an encoder, and not yet tested against the official client.
- **Work in progress, not working yet:** watching screen streams in the native desktop. Participants who share their screen show "AO VIVO" and can be clicked ("Assistir"); the bridge negotiates the stream over TS6 signaling and a webrtc-rs viewer decodes H.264 with Media Foundation into the stream view. Against the official TS6 client the connection does not complete yet (it ends with "rede bloqueada?"); findings and next steps are in `docs/persistent-chat-and-streaming-research.md`. Adds `webrtc`/`rtc` 0.21.1 and `windows` 0.62.2 to `ts6-bridge`, and the `ts6-cli stream-offer` diagnostic.
- Native desktop chat: drag files from Explorer onto the window to attach them; the paperclip sits left of the message field; the call area always leaves room for the chat at its current size; the connection popup shows the estimated voice delay again and the status bar's signal bars follow the ping color.
- Native desktop: the participant glow was removed and the grid picks the column count that gives the largest cards for the room; failed avatar downloads are retried after 30 seconds.
- Native desktop: only one Mirage runs per computer. A second launch restores and focuses the running window and exits, instead of attaching to the first window's bridge and sharing its session and identity. `--new-instance` opens an independent window ("Mirage (2)", up to 8 extra) with its own bridge port, profiles and TeamSpeak identity under `Mirage/slots/<n>`.
- Native desktop design pass: each participant glows in their own color behind the avatar (brighter while speaking); the connection popup is compact with right-aligned values; server rows select with a soft fill and bar instead of an outline, show their options button only when in use, and address redaction is quieter; the sidebar separates the live server from favorites and drops the duplicate add button; labels are sentence case and decimals use a comma (`2,0%`, `0,7 MB`).
- Native desktop: one sidebar for every screen. The connected server stays in a "Servidores" list with its channels folded under it, so the call no longer disappears on Inicio; the separate "Canais de voz" entry, the list/squares switch, the participant count and the three-dot menu are gone.
- Native desktop: the Convidar button copies a `ts3server://` link; the status bar is one button (connection, call time that resets per channel, ping graph, lost packets and percentage); chat rows align and no longer shift while hovered; channel chat is kept per server and channel on this computer and shown again after a restart (`profiles.json`, 200 messages per channel, removed with the server's history).
- Fix an inflated ping (80-100 ms instead of about 10 ms): replies were only read at the next 100 ms session wake-up, so the session now ticks every 10 ms. A vendored tsproto patch adds cumulative packet-loss counters (`client/patches/packet-loss-counts.patch`); `scripts/check_ping_latency.py` verifies the reported ping against a loopback server.
